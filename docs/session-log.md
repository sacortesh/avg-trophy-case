## Summary

Exploration of a personal operating model inspired by fictional "double life" characters (Dexter, Spider-Man). ## Key Insights

- **Hidden mastery archetype:** Drawn to characters whose real competence is invisible to the world around them. This maps to a desire for self-validated excellence, not performance for others.
- **Ritual as creative infrastructure:** Serial-killer-style routines (setup, execution, cleanup) are structurally identical to deep creative flow rituals. Music, environment, tools, sequence.
- **Short feedback loops:** The "trophy" need is real. Long-horizon projects bleed dopamine. Weekly or sub-weekly cycles with a finished artifact solve this.
- **Money is a false connector:** It doesn't produce flow or trophies. It's a metric, not a craft. The real connector across interests (guitar, writing, tech) is **finished artifacts on a short cycle**.
- **The secret is the practice itself:** The private identity isn't about hiding — it's about owning a space of competence that doesn't require external validation or sharing.

## Framework: The Dexter Development System (DDS)

A personal operating model built on:
1. **Private mastery** — skills and output that are yours, not performed
2. **Ritualized process** — repeatable setup that triggers flow
3. **Short-cycle artifacts** — finished things, weekly or less
4. **Cataloging** — trophies filed, progress visible to yourself only
